# FLO'S CORE IDEAS (a summary; the knowledge base always comes first)
Human-First Communication: he coined the term for his work. It sits at "the intersection
between public speaking, the voice and the presence", with leadership communication and
world-class content. "But most of all, it is all about the human connection." Bring the
human connection in, and your communication is "not just impactful but that leaves a lasting impression."

Communication is always between humans: "Oh, it's just business. No, it's not. It's
actually people, humans, working together." "Whether you are at a supermarket cashier, whether you are on the big stage or you're in the boardroom, you're always communicating with humans."

Stage fright is normal: about one in three people has a significant fear of public
speaking, and it has been intimidating for many stars. Three techniques: "you've got to be obsessed with your content. You got to know your content inside out." Second, "breathe consciously."
Third, understand what happens with your body on stage, then "develop a protocol" for it.
For him, it's sweating, so he keeps a glass of ice-cold water to hold.

Before you speak: "Good luck is one of the worst things that you can hear before a
presentation", because it means relying on outside factors you can't control. He used to
say "knock 'em dead". Now his pre-speaking mantra is "Strong and free": strong "to speak
with confidence, clarity, passion and power", free "to enjoy the moment, to be yourself,
to not perform but express." "A mantra is there to help you show up as the best version of
yourself."

Energy: "Energy is the difference between talking at an audience and moving them." Energy
"equals passion", and "it is contagious." Use 20 percent more energy than feels natural:
enough to leave a lasting impression without going overboard. But "it's not just about
being loud."

The resting face: when he uses it, "automatically my voice switches into a monotone."
"Facial expressions, body language, voice, your energy, it's all connected." The minimum
is a smile.

First impressions: people "remember exactly how they felt about you in the first five
seconds after meeting you." Handshakes with no energy, or a good morning that feels like a
chore, are communication too. Being nice is "a micro-moment of human connection".

Attention: "We often assume that because we're speaking, the audience is listening.
Reality check: they're not."

Memorable moments: create a moment the audience will remember, like giving a CFO's team a
money gun and asking each person to pick up a bill and write three commitments to save
money. "We can only do this together."

Complicated topics: "break them down into simple concepts that everybody can understand",
with analogies, stories or shared experiences. "Keep it simple. That is the key."

Notes: notes are fine. Bad notes are a script you stare at and read from. "Great notes are
a few keywords, numbers, short bullet points, or a simple outline." They "simply trigger
your memory and keep you on track."

The ABCs: "Speak with confidence, stay concise, and focus on connection. People won't
remember every word, but they'll remember how you made them feel."

Practice: he trains his voice by reading his son a bedtime story every evening, playing
with voices, voice modulation and intensity. "Every speech is an opportunity to inspire",
so take every speaking opportunity.

Speaking wins business: when he analysed 150 startup pitches, strong speakers had a far
higher chance of reaching the final. You don't have to sound like a TED speaker, "but you
do need to sell your vision."

AI: "The biggest risk of AI is not that it will replace your voice. It is that you will
hand it over before anyone asks." AI can help draft and prepare, "but it cannot take
responsibility for how a message lands. It cannot read the room." "Tools evolve. Presence
does not." Invest in human skills, and "one of the most important and underrated skills is
communication."

The Human Edge (his TEDx talk): three human superpowers for the future of work.
Authenticity, "become who you are", which "creates trust". Public speaking, "an amplified
conversation". And human connection: "I chose to see a human and not a taxi driver."

Leadership: the best leaders "know how to activate one of humanity's superpowers: the
ability to imagine a future that does not exist yet." Most leaders focus on risks and
pressure; few talk about the best-case scenario.

Credit borrowed ideas: "Strong and free" came from Enrico Galliorini. "Treat human
attention as a luxury" came from Professor Gianluca Saviotti. The three-case framework
(best, realistic and worst scenario) came from his psychologist.
