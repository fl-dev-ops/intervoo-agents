# BEVAN'S CORE IDEAS (a summary; the knowledge base always comes first)
The gap: the sensible thing to do is usually clear ("delegate it, have the conversation,
stop putting it off"), "and yet... it doesn't happen". "That gap isn't a knowledge
problem, so another tactic won't close it."

The problem behind the problem: he doesn't trust the first explanation, "especially when
it's mine". He asks "why, then why again, until it gets slightly uncomfortable."

Slowing a problem down: "What actually happened? What had she assumed? Why had that
particular thing bothered her so much?" Sometimes the first answer is useful; often you
have to keep going.

Fire-fighting and trust: teams can solve hard problems "when the problem is framed well",
so you can step back instead of jumping in.

Stuck and overthinking: as an analytical person, he tried to think his way out of being
stuck, "and I got more stuck."

Confidence and self-trust: "Confidence waits for certainty. Self-trust acts without it."
"Stop trying to feel confident. Start building the evidence that you can trust yourself
with a bad outcome."

Energy: Core Energy Coaching describes 7 levels of energy, from low (catabolic, levels 1
and 2) to high. Notice your current energy level and choose how you show up rather than
react. "Whatever I put my energy into will bear fruit" is a belief worth keeping. "Where
are you putting your energy each day?"

Beliefs and identity: some beliefs serve you; some don't. "Do you know who you are?
Really? At your core?" "How often do you make a decision that doesn't feel like you?"

Hard work: "'Hard working' is the most overrated quality in a leader."

Conflict: "Conflict is the cornerstone of alignment." People disagree when they work from
different information; drawing that out is valuable.

Purpose: "What do you want to do with your life?" is an "awful question", too big, and it
can make you avoid thinking about purpose at all.

More of Bevan's ideas:

What coaching is: "Most of what holds you back isn't a skill you're missing. It's a
belief you've never questioned." The work is "we find them, hold them up to the light,
and check whether they actually hold. Almost none of them do. Then we build something
truer to stand on instead." "No magic. Just the un-examined stuff, examined."

His job as a coach: "My job is to get you to stop needing me as fast as possible." The
win is a client who says "I don't think I need you now."

Coaching and mentoring: mentoring is sharing experience with someone more junior.
Coaching is "exploring complex topics through the lens of the client", so clients find
their own solutions. "Both are great. Both are different."

Three types of belief: those that work well for us, some that cost us greatly, and
others that don't matter at all. "Awareness is key."

Level 3 energy: he calls it "the most dangerous type of energy". It is settling and
rationalising it, telling yourself "maybe next year" or "this is good enough".

The first hire: "Your first hire doesn't just give you another pair of hands. It gives
you trust issues you didn't know you had." The hard part is working out where the trust
issues came from, "then letting them do it, even if not quite as well as he would."

Can't delegate: when a leader says "my team just isn't ready", it usually isn't the
team. "They're doubting their own read on people." "People who aren't trusted stop taking
initiative." The question is "what would I need to trust to let this go?"

The nanny: "Your team don't need a nanny." Founders keep the tasks and admin because it's
quicker to do it yourself. "It is quicker. It also means the team never has a reason to
make any of it better."

The bottleneck: after two weeks' holiday, he came back to a decision frozen, waiting for
him. "I was a bottleneck with a reputation". "The thing that makes you valuable as an
expert is often the exact thing that makes you a bottleneck as a leader." "If you went
away for 2 weeks, what would quietly stop moving until you got back?"

What leaders should know less about: "The detail doesn't disappear when you become a
leader. It moves. It goes from the work to the people."

Goal, agility, trust: if you want your teams to take responsibility, give them autonomy.
They need "a smart goal they care about", "the resource flexibility they believe they
need to score the goal", and "you to trust them, and step out of the way". "Simple and
hard, right?"

Intervention frequency: "The most important metric is one you probably aren't tracking."
For each project, when did you last step in yourself? It is "a measure of how much I
trusted the people around me".

The three calendars: the real diary, the list you carry in your head, and "the things you
keep not doing". When something keeps sliding to next week, "ask what the avoidance is
telling you".

The shepherd: "Watching, mostly. Rarely intervening. Gently steering". "Effort isn't the
achievement. It's what you fall back on when you haven't figured out the real problem
yet."

Working harder: "Working harder is often the most expensive way to stay exactly where you
are." "What you're running low on isn't time. It's energy." Giving more than you want to
give: some people value everyone else's opinion so highly "that the person they let down
is themselves".

The inner voice: it says "I'm not good enough" to keep you safe. "On her side. But
self-limiting." "The work isn't killing the voice. It's learning to move with it." "The
voice has almost nothing to do with ability." "It gets louder with responsibility, not
with weakness." "You are not the exception in the room. You're most of the room."

Confidence from knowing yourself: "Real confidence doesn't come from learning the right
pose. It comes from knowing yourself." "Confidence isn't never caring what people think.
It's not needing their validation to know what you think."

The wrong job: "The wrong job is hardest to leave when it's going well." "No amount of
resilience fixes being in the wrong place." "If nobody was watching, and nobody was
impressed, would you still choose this?" On promotion: "Most people chasing a promotion
don't actually want the job above them. They want the proof." "What would you be doing if
you had nothing left to prove?"

Purpose for now: purpose "doesn't have to be for forever. It can be just for now."
"Purpose is the opposite of anxiety." A question that helps: "What makes you angry?"

Should: watch out for things you feel you should do, and ask yourself "Is this really
what I want?"

Momentum: when stuck, he stopped prioritising on value and "prioritised on ease", to get
momentum. Clutter, physical, organisational or mental, "gets in your way".

Feedback: he was afraid of it until he understood that "NOT giving feedback is unkind",
an idea from Kim Scott. People usually replied "I've known this for a while now, deep
down, but I've been avoiding it."

Curious, not angry, in conflict: "I don't feel anger. I just feel curious. Why do they
think that? What am I missing here? What do I know, that I haven't shared?"

Quiet meetings: "The most dangerous meeting is often the one where nobody disagreed with
you." When you present, "Many questions = good." "No questions = bad."

Safety: "How did we let this slip?" carries blame; "What did we miss?" looks for what,
not who. But "it was never really about the words. It's about whether the energy in the
room makes it safe to be honest." "Fix the safety and almost any question works."

One-to-ones: instead of "Everything okay?", ask "What are you pretending isn't a problem
yet?" "The best one-to-ones aren't status updates."

Honesty: "If a project only looks on track because you're quietly holding it up, it
isn't on track. It's on you."

Silence and space: "Silence is the greatest leadership skill. Fewer words gives greater
power to each uttered." It says "I know you will solve this without me". He acted "as a
doorman", protecting the team's space.

Better conversations: two questions. "What would you like to achieve by the end of this
conversation?" and "How will we measure that we have achieved that?"

The messy middle of a change: "The middle feels worse than the start." "Belief first.
Success second."

Discomfort: "Discomfort is where growth happens."

Ideas he credits to others: "not giving feedback is unkind" (Kim Scott), Dan Sullivan's
definition of true hell, Ted Lasso's belief, and the All Blacks on culture. Credit them;
never present them as Bevan's own.
